## 2. Is the 4.2% CPI supply-side or demand-side? → **~85–90% supply-side. Here's the arithmetic.**

CPI accelerated from 2.33% (May 2025) to 4.27% (May 2026) = **+1.94pp of acceleration**.

- Energy (≈6.7% of the CPI basket) swung from −3.6% YoY to +22.1% YoY. That swing alone contributes **≈ +1.7pp** — roughly **85–90% of the entire acceleration**.
- Core CPI went 2.78% → 2.96% over the same year. Essentially flat.
- Core **goods** are at +1.03% YoY and just **+0.11% 3m-annualized** — the tariff cost-push impulse of 2025 is *fading*, not building. (May core commodities actually fell 0.1% m/m.)
- Shelter (3.62%, decelerating from 3.99%) is the usual lagged component, not new pressure.

**Why it's not demand-pull — the checklist a demand-driven inflation would show, and what we actually see:**

| Demand-pull signature | Present? | Evidence |
|---|---|---|
| Wages accelerating above inflation | ❌ | Wages +3.45% and *decelerating*; real wages negative |
| Labor market tightening | ❌ | U-6 at 8.1%, long-term unemployed +524K YoY |
| Core inflation broadening | ❌ | Core 3m-ann 3.17%, core goods ~0% |
| Real consumption running hot | ❌ | Retail sales +4.9% nominal − 4.3% CPI ≈ +0.6% real |
| Policy stimulus pumping demand | ❌ | Fed had been *cutting* into a slowing economy; no new fiscal impulse |

This is a textbook **exogenous supply shock (energy) sitting on top of a mildly-too-warm core**, with a *secondary* cost-push layer (tariffs, now fading) and almost no demand-pull. The closest historical rhyme is **1990 (Gulf War: oil doubled, CPI hit 6.3%, demand was soft, inflation collapsed back to 3% by 1992)** and **mid-2008 (oil $147, headline CPI 5.6% while the labor market was already cracking)** — *not* 2021-22 (which had $5T of fiscal transfers, +6% wage growth, and core exploding).

**The one genuinely worrying wrinkle:** Michigan long-run expectations jumped 3.5% → 3.9% in one month, with consumers saying they expect inflation to "proliferate beyond fuel." Supply shocks are harmless *if expectations hold* (1990) and catastrophic *if they don't* (1973). That single number is what decides whether this is Scenario B or Scenario C below.

**One more nuance: the AI buildout itself is currently a *supply-side inflation contributor*.** Datacenter electricity demand is pushing utility costs, grid buildout, and construction labor — capex booms are inflationary while building and deflationary only after (railway iron prices spiked during the 1840s mania; the cheap freight came later). The promised AI productivity disinflation is a 2027+ story.

## 3. The AI capex bubble — how much more can investors keep it going?

Think of it as four fuel tanks. Two are still full, two are draining fast.

**Tank 1 — Macro intensity (FULL).** AI-specific capex ≈ 0.8–1.0% of GDP. Historical tech-boom *peaks*: telecom 2000 ≈ 1.2%, US railroads 1880s ≈ 2–4%, UK railway mania 1847 ≈ 7%. At +36%/yr growth, AI capex hits telecom-peak intensity around **late 2027–2028**. On this measure alone, the boom is mid-cycle, not terminal.

**Tank 2 — Valuation (MOSTLY FULL).** AI cohort at ~25× forward earnings with 38% projected EPS CAGR — rich but nothing like March-2000 Nasdaq (Cisco 130×, telecoms at 60×+ with fake revenue). The earnings are still real. Concentration (45% of SPX) is the fragility, not the multiple.

**Tank 3 — Funding mix (DRAINING — this is the late-cycle tell).** The boom has crossed from cash-funded to credit-funded: $100B of big-tech bonds YTD, record CDS protection demand, half of the $3T 2025-28 datacenter pipeline on private credit, limited-recourse SPVs designed to push losses onto the financial system, and ~$800B of circular deals (NVDA→OpenAI→Oracle→back). This is **exactly** the telecom 1999 pattern — Lucent/Nortel vendor financing made demand look organic until it wasn't. OpenAI losing ~$14B in 2026 while being the demand anchor for hundreds of billions of capex is the Global-Crossing-shaped hole in the story.

**Tank 4 — The discount rate (DRAINING, and it's the binding one).** Bubbles rarely die of valuation; they die when the funding cost rises above the dream (1929, 1989 Japan, 2000 — all killed by central banks). The 4.2% CPI just **welded the Fed's relief valve shut**: cuts priced for Q3/Q4-2026 are sliding to 2027, under a brand-new chair (Warsh) who must prove credibility. Every month energy stays high, the bubble's carry cost stays up while hyperscaler FCF goes negative.

**Bottom line on runway: roughly 12–24 more months on the base case.** The GDP-intensity and valuation tanks support continuation into 2027; the credit and rates tanks say the *quality* of the advance degrades from here (narrower, more debt-fueled, more circular). The thing to watch is not the equity tape — it's **HY OAS at 2.78%**: spreads that tight mean credit sees no problem yet, and bubbles don't end while credit is wide open. The end begins when a neocloud/SPV misses a payment and private-credit marks get questioned, or when the Fed is forced to *hike*.

## 4. Five Scenarios — best to worst

### Scenario A — "1995 Productivity Soft Landing" (best case, ~15%)
Iran de-escalates, energy unwinds (energy base effects turn *negative* by mid-2027), AI productivity gains start showing in the data, capex glides to a plateau funded by genuinely growing cash flows.
- **Markets:** bubble matures rather than pops; broadening rally; 1995-99 analog where Greenspan held rates despite 4% unemployment because productivity ran 2.5-3%.
- **Inflation:** **disinflationary** — headline back to ~2.5% within a year (energy math), core grinds down as AI cuts unit labor costs. The "good deflation" of supply expansion: 1920s electrification, post-1850 railways.
- **Validation:** 1995-99 — the only era where a capex boom ended without a bust; core PCE *fell* from 2.3% to 1.5% during the boom.

### Scenario B — "Orderly Capex Digestion" (base case, ~30%)
Energy fades (1990-style) but AI ROI questions force hyperscalers to cut 2027 capex guidance 20–30%. Equity correction concentrated in the AI complex (−15 to −25% on SPX given 45% weight), no credit cascade because the big balance sheets absorb it.
- **Markets:** 2022-style derating; capex stocks (SMH, power, datacenter REITs) hit hardest; broad market holds.
- **Inflation:** **mildly disinflationary** — capex pullback removes ~0.5-1% of GDP demand, energy base effects help, Fed *gets its cuts back* in 2027. Headline 2-2.5% by late 2027.
- **Validation:** 1990-92 (oil shock faded, CPI 6.3%→3.0% in 18 months); 2015-16 energy capex bust (US E&P capex −50%, contained because banks weren't the core lenders — the difference now is private credit *is* the core lender, which is what separates B from D).

### Scenario E — "1999 Melt-Up Finale" (best near-term, worst ending, ~20%)
Political pressure (the administration is openly inflation-tolerant) plus a softening labor market push Warsh into premature cuts while CPI is still 3.5-4%. Liquidity floods into the narrowest, strongest trend — AI. Parabolic 6-18 month blowoff: another +30-60% on the AI complex before terminal break.
- **Markets:** Nasdaq-1999 redux (+86% in the final year, then −78% over the next two).
- **Inflation:** **THIS is the scenario that creates true demand-pull inflation.** Wealth effects + cheap money broaden inflation beyond energy: 4.2% supply-side becomes 5-6% demand-side. The inflation *type mutates* from cost-push to liquidity-driven demand-pull.
- **Validation:** Sep-1998 LTCM cuts → 1999 melt-up → 2000 bust (CPI rose 1.6%→3.4% during the melt-up); 1927 Fed easing → 1929. Both times the "insurance cuts" fueled the terminal phase and forced harder tightening after.

### Scenario C — "Stagflation Grind" (~22%)
Iran conflict persists, energy stays elevated 12+ months, and the Michigan long-run number (already 3.9%) keeps climbing — expectations de-anchor. Fed forced to stay on hold or *hike* into a weakening labor market. The bubble isn't shot; it's **suffocated** — funding costs stay high while AI FCF turns negative, private credit stops extending, capex rolls over slowly.
- **Markets:** grinding bear, multiple compression on the long-duration AI trade; 1973-74 Nifty Fifty analog (one-decision stocks −60-80%, SPX −48% over two years).
- **Inflation:** **cost-push mutating into expectations-embedded inflation** — the dangerous 1970s transformation, where a supply shock becomes self-sustaining via expectations even without union COLA mechanics. Headline sticky at 4-5%, core drifts to 3.5%+.
- **Validation:** 1973-74 (OPEC shock on a market already priced for perfection: CPI 3.4%→12.3%, Fed funds to 13%, Nifty Fifty destroyed). Mitigant vs the 70s: 6% unionization vs 25%, no COLA contracts, anchored *market* breakevens so far — which is why this gets a grind, not a 12% spiral.

### Scenario D — "Credit-Event Bust" (worst case, ~13%)
A neocloud or datacenter SPV chain defaults (the limited-recourse structures mean losses land on private credit funds, not Microsoft). Marks get questioned across the $1.5T private-credit datacenter book, lending freezes, hyperscalers slash capex simultaneously, NVDA hits a revenue air pocket, and the 45%-AI-weighted index has no shock absorber. SPX −35-50%.
- **Markets:** 2000-02 telecom playbook ($2T of telecom market value erased, capex −50% in two years, Nasdaq −78%) with a 2008-style private-credit accelerant.
- **Inflation:** **deflationary bust** — demand destruction overwhelms the energy shock. 2001: CPI 3.7%→1.1%. 2008: CPI 5.6% (July, oil $147) → **−2.1%** (July 2009). Energy collapses on demand fears; the 4.2% problem solves itself brutally.
- **Second act (validated by 2009-2021→2022):** the policy response — emergency cuts, QE, fiscal rescue of private credit — seeds the *next* demand-pull inflation cycle 2-3 years later, exactly as the COVID response produced 9.1% CPI. Deflation first, reflation trade after.
- **Validation:** 2000-02 telecom (vendor financing = today's circular deals, almost line for line), 2008 (energy-spike-on-credit-fragility = today's exact configuration), 1929 (capex boom + leverage + Fed tightening into it).

## 7. Bottom line

1. **The 4.2% CPI is ~85-90% an energy supply shock** (Iran conflict) on top of a stable ~3% core. Wages decelerating below inflation and rising labor slack rule out demand-pull. Closest rhymes: 1990 and mid-2008 — both energy spikes over a softening economy, and they resolved in opposite directions (1990 faded; 2008 met a credit bust).
2. **The AI bubble has macro room (capex ~1% of GDP vs 1.2-2%+ historical peaks, multiples nothing like 2000) but its funding quality is degrading fast** — debt, private credit, SPVs, circular deals are the telecom-1999 fingerprint. Base-case runway ≈ 12-24 months, contingent on credit staying open (HY OAS 2.78% says it still is) and the Fed not hiking.
3. **The cruel coupling:** the supply shock that isn't the bubble's fault is what holds rates up and drains the bubble's fuel. The single most important variable for *both* questions is the same one — **whether energy persists long enough to de-anchor expectations** (Michigan long-run 3.9% is the tripwire).
4. History says supply shocks fade (1990) unless policy errors entrench them (1973) — and capex bubbles end via credit, not valuation (2000, 2008). Watch the credit tape, not the equity tape.

**Did big names get slack when IPO'ing into strained tapes? Post-IPO watch: revenue deceleration prints + capital access, not profitability.

**Race-to-the-bottom precedents (LLM price war — "none can profit, one must fall"):** Pattern = marginal cost ≪ fixed cost + undifferentiated product → prices below full cost; ends ONLY via capital-window closure → weakest-FUNDED (not smallest) exits → consolidation → survivors get pricing power. (1) Railroads 1870s-93: rate wars, freight −70%; Panic of 1893 put 1/3 of US mileage in receivership (the giants); Morgan consolidation ended the war. (2) DRAM (closest analog — bits=tokens, commodity, huge fixed capex): Intel EXITED 1985 (creator couldn't profit); ~20 makers → 3 (Samsung/Hynix/Micron); price-per-bit fell forever AND volume grew forever — profit only post-consolidation for lowest-cost survivors. First out = the one whose parent stopped funding, not the smallest. (3) Telecom bandwidth 1996-2002: −90% pricing; leveraged pure-plays died, cross-subsidized incumbents absorbed assets. (4) Streaming: Netflix Apr-2022 miss repriced the WHOLE sector in one quarter → industry-wide pivot to profit. Ride-share: 8-yr subsidy war ended only when rates rose 2021-22. Counter-example = cloud (AWS/Azure/GCP): same capex, but switching costs → rational profitable oligopoly, no race. **AI question: is intelligence DRAM or cloud?** Token prices/capability already −100x since 2023 (race is ON; why OpenAI loses $14B on big revenue) but distribution/enterprise integration adds cloud-like stickiness at the top. Failure screen (weakest funding + least differentiated + no profitable parent): xAI most exposed (thin enterprise revenue, single-patron funding; SpaceX IPO = patron monetizing one asset to fund another — vendor-financing-shaped); Meta/Google = the AT&Ts (cross-subsidize forever); OpenAI/Anthropic middle (no parent, but best compounding + post-IPO public capital access). Kicker: in every precedent the FIRST major failure repriced the entire sector (1893, Global Crossing, Netflix Q1-22) — one lab failing = the day markets demand profit from everyone = capex bids cut = the NVDA chain reprices. Bridge from "one falls" to Scenario B-or-D even with oil resolved.
