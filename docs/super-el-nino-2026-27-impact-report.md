# Super El Niño 2026–27 — Measurement, Tracking & Commodity/Natural-Gas Impact Report

*Prepared 2026-06-10. Focus: US commodities + global natural-gas demand, with US and India as the two anchor markets. All figures sourced; see Sources section.*

---

## 0. How to read this report (the one thing that matters)

There is a **central paradox** you must internalize before any trade or hedge:

> **A super El Niño is structurally BEARISH for US natural gas and structurally BULLISH for Indian natural gas/LNG — simultaneously.**

- **US gas ↓**: El Niño = warm US winters = fewer heating-degree-days = weaker heating demand = storage glut = lower Henry Hub. In strong El Niño winters US heating-degree-days run **~7% below** non-El-Niño years; gas fell **72% Feb-2014→Feb-2016** through the last super event ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=25952), [Yahoo/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/el-ni-o-la-nina-152627409.html)).
- **India gas ↑**: El Niño = weak monsoon = drought = collapsing hydro + brutal heatwaves = surging air-conditioning load = gas/coal fill the gap = more LNG imports. In H1-2024 (tail of the 2023-24 event) Indian **hydro fell 8% YoY** while **gas-fired output jumped 50%** ([Zerodha Daily Brief](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/a-little-boy-comes-for-indias-monsoons)).

So "the impact of super El Niño on natural gas" has **no single sign** — it depends entirely on which market and which transmission channel. The rest of this report measures both channels and gives you the tiered, trackable framework you asked for.

## 1. Definition — what makes an El Niño a "super" El Niño

| Term | Definition | Source |
|---|---|---|
| **El Niño (present)** | 3-month-averaged SST anomaly in the **Niño-3.4 region** (5°N–5°S, 120°W–170°W) **≥ +0.5 °C** = the Oceanic Niño Index (ONI) | [NOAA Climate.gov](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/understanding-climate/climate-variability-oceanic-nino-index) |
| **Strong** | ONI peak **≥ +1.5 °C** | NOAA ONI scale |
| **Very strong / "Super"** | ONI peak **≥ +2.0 °C** (3-month average). *No formal "super" definition exists — it is the informal label for a ≥+2.0 °C, "very strong" event* | [Yale Climate Connections](https://yaleclimateconnections.org/2026/05/whats-a-super-el-nino-and-other-el-nino-questions-answered/), [KSU Agronomy](https://eupdate.agronomy.ksu.edu/article/what-is-a-super-el-nino-and-how-will-one-potentially-affect-kansas-571-2) |

**Key nuance**: "super" is a *peak-intensity* label, not a separate phenomenon. The threshold to watch is **ONI crossing +2.0 °C on a 3-month average**. For reference, 2015-16 peaked at ONI **+2.1 °C** ("very strong").

| **1982-83** | ~+2.1 °C | One of strongest 20th-c. events; major global ag disruption |
| **1997-98** | ~+2.4 °C | Strongest of the century. US residential gas **−5.6%** vs prior winter; storage **+69% YoY** to 1,472 Bcf; gas **−72%** peak-to-trough ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=25952)). Palm-oil yields **−5-10%** → vegetable-oil prices up |

## 4. The trackable stages — how to measure whether it's unfolding "as expected"

This is your **monitoring dashboard**. El Niño develops through a known causal chain; each link is independently observable, so you can confirm or falsify the forecast in near-real-time. Watch them in this order ([NOAA Climate.gov](https://www.climate.gov/news-features/blogs/enso/predicting-el-ni%C3%B1o-then-and-now), [NOAA PMEL 1997-98 anatomy](https://www.pmel.noaa.gov/pubs/outstand/mcph2029/mcph2029.shtml)):

| # | Stage / indicator | What confirms escalation | Where to track | Cadence |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Subsurface heat content** (upper 150 m equatorial Pacific) | Large positive anomaly = "fuel in the tank"; precondition seen >1 yr before 1997-98 | NOAA CPC subsurface / TAO array | Weekly |
| 2 | **Trade winds / Westerly Wind Bursts (WWBs)** | Easterlies weaken or reverse; WWBs over W. Pacific = trigger | CPC, BoM | Weekly |
| 3 | **Downwelling Kelvin waves** | Warm subsurface pulse crosses Pacific W→E (~2 months transit), surfaces in E. Pacific | NASA/NOAA Hovmöller plots | Bi-weekly |
| 4 | **Niño-3.4 SST / ONI** | The headline. Crossing **+1.5** = strong; **+2.0** = super | NOAA ONI table, weekly SST | Weekly / monthly ONI |
| 5 | **SOI (Southern Oscillation Index)** | Sustained **negative** SOI = atmosphere "coupling" to the warm ocean (the self-reinforcing phase) | BoM, CPC | Daily/30-day |
| 6 | **OLR / convection shift** | Suppressed convection over Indonesia, enhanced over central Pacific | CPC OLR | Weekly |
| 7 | **Atmospheric coupling lock-in** | Walker circulation reorganizes; event becomes self-sustaining | CPC ENSO discussion (monthly, 2nd Thu) | Monthly |

**Falsification signals** (the "El No-Show" risks): Kelvin waves fade without atmospheric coupling; SOI fails to go persistently negative; subsurface warmth dissipates before surfacing. If by **Aug–Sep 2026** the ONI is *not* climbing toward +1.0 and SOI is *not* persistently negative, downgrade the "super" thesis.

## 5. US commodity impacts

### 5a. US Natural Gas — **BEARISH** (the load-bearing US conclusion)

**Mechanism**: El Niño → warmer-than-normal US winter (esp. North) → fewer heating-degree-days → lower residential/commercial heating draw → storage stays full → downward price pressure.

**Validated magnitudes**:
- Strong-El-Niño winters: US heating-degree-days **~7% below** non-El-Niño average ([Yahoo/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/el-ni-o-la-nina-152627409.html)).
- 2015-16: residential gas **−5.6%** vs prior winter; working gas in storage **1,472 Bcf, +69% YoY** ([EIA](https://www.eia.gov/todayinenergy/detail.php?id=25952)).
- 2014→2016 (into the super event): Henry Hub **−72%** peak-to-trough ([Yahoo/Zacks](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/el-ni-o-la-nina-152627409.html)).
- Confirmed by [CME Group](https://www.cmegroup.com/insights/economic-research/2023/as-el-nino-intensifies-natural-gas-takes-a-tumble.html) and [BloombergNEF](https://about.bnef.com/insights/commodities/el-nino-foreshadows-new-challenges-to-global-gas-market/) for the 2023-24 cycle.

**Caveat (2026 is not 2015)**: the structural backdrop has shifted. US **LNG export capacity** is far larger now, which tightens the domestic balance and partially offsets a warm-winter glut — a warm winter that would have crushed prices in 2016 has a firmer floor in 2026-27 because surplus gas can be exported. **Net**: directional bias still bearish for Henry Hub on a confirmed super event, but the magnitude is likely *smaller* than the 2015-16 analog. Treat the historical −5-70% range as an upper bound, not a forecast.

### 5b. US Agriculture & Softs — **MIXED, commodity-specific**

El Niño does **not** move ag uniformly; sign depends on crop and region ([Tandfonline](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/1331677X.2023.2186913), [ECB](https://www.ecb.europa.eu/press/economic-bulletin/focus/2023/html/ecb.ebbox202306_01~36e78cc75e.en.html), [Alpha-Ex Capital](https://www.alphaexcapital.com/commodities/agricultural-and-soft-commodities/weather-and-crop-reports-in-agriculture/enso-impact-on-agriculture)):

| Commodity | El Niño bias | Mechanism |
|---|---|---|
| **US Soybeans** | Neutral-to-↑ yield (price ↓) | El Niño often *favors* US soy harvests; bearish price |
| **US Corn** | ↓ yield / ↑ price risk | Negative effect on US corn yields; ENSO drives ±5-8% Midwest price swings |
| **US Wheat** | ↓ yield / ↑ price risk | Negative effect on US wheat yields |
| **Coffee** | ↓ price | El Niño conducive to production → lower coffee prices |
| **Sugar** | ↑ price | Upward pressure (Asia/Brazil cane stress) |
| **Cocoa** | ↑ price | Upward pressure (W. Africa dryness) |
| **Rice / food oils** | ↑ price | Upward pressure |
| **Palm oil (Indonesia/Malaysia)** | ↑ price | Yields **−5-10%** depending on severity; 2015-16 pushed global veg-oil up |

**Global framing**: 1997-98 cut harvested food crops **−6.4%** with ag prices trending **up** overall. The ECB and Banco de España both find El Niño exerts measurable **upward pressure on the global food-commodity aggregate**, concentrated in softs, rice, and edible oils — partially offset by US grain resilience.

## 6. India focus — **BULLISH natural gas / LNG** (the inverse channel)

India is the cleanest example of El Niño being *bullish* for gas demand, via a four-step chain:

**Step 1 — Monsoon failure.** ~60% of El Niño years → below-average Indian rainfall (1951-2022). 2015-16: monsoon **86% of LPA**. ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/news/super-el-nino-explained-india-monsoon-impact))

**Step 2 — Hydro collapse + heatwave.** 2023-24 El Niño → hottest year on record for India (+0.65 °C); H1-2024 **hydro −8% YoY** as catchments dried. ([Zerodha](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/a-little-boy-comes-for-indias-monsoons))

**Step 3 — Power-demand surge.** Peak demand has structurally exploded: **154 GW (2015) → 246 GW (2024), +59% in a decade**; govt projected **270 GW** for summer 2024. Apr–Jun 2024 electricity demand ran **+10.8% YoY**, AC load ~⅓ of the May increase. ([Zerodha](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/a-little-boy-comes-for-indias-monsoons))

**Step 4 — Gas/LNG fills the gap.** Gas plants ramp fast, so they're the swing supply when hydro fails:
- H1-2024 **gas-fired output +50% YoY** ([Zerodha](https://thedailybrief.zerodha.com/p/a-little-boy-comes-for-indias-monsoons)).
- Jan–Oct 2023: power-sector **RLNG consumption +128% YoY** to 7.4 MMcm/d, as peak demand hit 243 GW (+12.5%) ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/lng/121924-commodities-2025-indias-lng-imports-to-rise-4-10-amid-boost-in-demand-policy-support)).
- India is now the **4th-largest LNG importer**; **36 bcm imported in 2024**; spot cargoes ~doubled to >110 in 2024 to plug demand-vs-domestic-supply gap ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/india-gas-market-report/executive-summary), [S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/lng/121924-commodities-2025-indias-lng-imports-to-rise-4-10-amid-boost-in-demand-policy-support)).
- Structural trajectory: LNG demand **→64 bcm/yr by 2030 (~11%/yr)** with flat domestic output ([IEA](https://www.iea.org/reports/india-gas-market-report/executive-summary)).

**El Niño-specific increment**: a confirmed super event in 2026-27 layers a drought-driven, heat-driven *spike* on top of this structural ~11% growth — expect **above-trend spot LNG buying through the 2027 pre-monsoon/summer window (Mar–Jun 2027)** and the cleanest tradeable signal. The price-sensitivity caveat: India is **price-elastic on spot LNG** — if global LNG (JKM/TTF) spikes, Indian buyers defer spot and lean on coal, capping the import surge ([S&P Global](https://www.spglobal.com/commodity-insights/en/news-research/latest-news/lng/122823-indias-lng-imports-set-for-7-8-boost-in-2024-on-hopes-of-softer-prices)).

**The global tension**: US warm-winter gas glut (bearish Henry Hub) can coincide with — and partly *supply* — the Indian/Asian LNG pull (bullish JKM). The Henry-Hub-to-JKM spread is where the two channels meet.

## 7. Tiered impact framework (low → medium → high) — keyed to ONI peak

You asked for varied impact levels. Calibrate to **realized ONI peak**, because impact scales non-linearly with intensity:

| Tier | ONI peak | Probability-weighted? | US Nat-Gas | US Ag/Softs | India Gas/LNG |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **LOW** | +0.5 to +1.0 (weak) | Possible if event under-delivers | Modest warm-winter bias; −0 to −5% HDD effect | Minimal, noise-level | Monsoon near-normal; structural growth only |
| **MEDIUM** | +1.0 to +1.5 (moderate) | Base case if "super" fails | HDD −3-5%; storage builds; HH soft | Softs (sugar/cocoa/oils) firm; grains mixed | Monsoon ~90-95% LPA; modest hydro dip; LNG +5-8% above trend |
| **HIGH** | +1.5 to +2.0 (strong) | ~67% reach here-or-above | HDD −5-7%; clear HH downside (capped by LNG exports) | Broad softs rally; veg-oil +; corn/wheat yield risk | Monsoon 85-90% LPA; hydro −5-10%; heatwave; gas gen +30-50%; spot LNG surge |
| **EXTREME** | **≥ +2.0 ("super")** | The headline scenario | Strongest warm-winter glut; biggest HH downside risk | 1997-98 analog: food-crop −6%, ag-aggregate up | 2015-16 analog: monsoon ~86% LPA, drought, record heat, max LNG pull (price-elasticity permitting) |

## 8. Monitoring checklist — your running scorecard

Track monthly; each "yes" raises conviction that the EXTREME/HIGH tier is materializing:

- [ ] **ONI** climbing toward +1.5 by Sep–Oct 2026, +2.0 by DJF 2026-27 *(the master gauge)*
- [ ] **Weekly Niño-3.4** sustained > +1.0 through summer 2026 (currently +0.9)
- [ ] **SOI** persistently **negative** (atmospheric coupling confirmed)
- [ ] **Subsurface heat content** anomaly large & positive through mid-2026
- [ ] **Downwelling Kelvin wave(s)** crossing to E. Pacific (NASA Hovmöller)
- [ ] **US HDD forecasts** (CPC winter outlook, released Oct 2026) trending below normal
- [ ] **EIA weekly gas storage** building above 5-yr average into winter 2026-27
- [ ] **India IMD monsoon forecast** (released ~Apr/May 2027) below 96% LPA
- [ ] **India peak-power & RLNG/spot-LNG** prints rising YoY (PPAC monthly data)
- [ ] **JKM–Henry Hub spread** widening (the cross-market El Niño signature)

## 9. Bottom line

1. **The event is real and near-certain to emerge; "super" (≥+2.0 °C) is ~67% likely but not locked** — peak intensity is the one genuinely uncertain variable, and the forecast sits in the low-skill spring-barrier window. Re-verify monthly via ONI/SOI.
2. **There is no single "natural gas" answer.** US Henry Hub = **bearish** (warm winter, glut), magnitude *muted vs 2015-16* by the larger US LNG-export buffer. Indian gas/LNG = **bullish** (drought → hydro loss → heat → gas-fired ramp → spot LNG), subject to spot-price elasticity.
3. **US commodities are commodity-specific**: gas/coffee/soy bias down; sugar/cocoa/rice/edible-oils bias up; corn/wheat yield-risk up. The aggregate food basket biases **up** in a true super event (1997-98 precedent).
4. **The cleanest tradeable expressions** are (a) the **JKM–Henry Hub spread** (captures both channels at once), (b) **softs/veg-oils** on the EXTREME tier, and (c) **India LNG-import / gas-power** exposure into the **Mar–Jun 2027** pre-monsoon heat window.
5. **Falsify early**: if ONI isn't climbing and SOI isn't negative by Aug–Sep 2026, downgrade to MEDIUM tier and shrink the thesis.
